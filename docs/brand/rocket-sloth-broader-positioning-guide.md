# Rocket Sloth Broader Positioning Guide

## Core position

Rocket Sloth Consulting is a Texas-based technology partner for businesses of every kind. The brand should feel especially approachable to blue-collar, rural, and country-minded owners without suggesting that those are the only clients it serves.

Short version: country friendly, blue-collar friendly, business universal.

## Recommended homepage hero

Eyebrow:

> TEXAS-BASED WEBSITES & BUSINESS SYSTEMS

Headline:

> Websites and business systems that work as hard as you do.

Supporting copy:

> Rocket Sloth Consulting helps businesses of every kind build a stronger online presence, capture better leads, and simplify customer follow-up without big-agency prices or tech-industry jargon.

Primary button:

> Let's Talk About Your Business

Secondary button:

> Explore Services

Trust line:

> Practical technology. Personal service. Built around the way you work.

## Replace the ranch-only homepage section

Replace "Website and marketing services built for Texas Longhorn ranches" with:

> Practical technology for businesses that keep things moving.

Replace the supporting paragraph with:

> From contractors and rural businesses to retailers, restaurants, and professional service firms, we build websites and systems around how your business actually works.

Suggested category line:

> Home and field services | Local shops and retail | Hospitality | Professional services | Agriculture and rural business

Keep Longhorn websites in the Specializations navigation as proof of industry expertise, not as the homepage's main identity.

## Recommended about-page language

Section headline:

> Blue-collar and country friendly. Business friendly, period.

Supporting copy:

> Rocket Sloth feels at home in a shop, on a jobsite, at a ranch, behind a counter, or in a conference room. The goal is the same: practical technology that makes your business easier to find, easier to run, and easier to grow.

## Visual mix

For the main site, aim for roughly 50 percent mixed/general business imagery, 30 percent blue-collar or service-business imagery, and 20 percent rural or country imagery. Avoid using more than one ranch-specific photo on a general page. The Longhorn specialization page can remain fully ranch-focused.

## Voice guardrails

- Lead with usefulness, clarity, ownership, and personal service.
- Say "businesses of every kind" or "businesses that value practical results" instead of "any and all businesses."
- Use Texas and country cues as personality, not as eligibility requirements.
- Keep industry examples varied whenever more than one example appears.
- Avoid Silicon Valley jargon, corporate stock language, and language that implies office-based clients are less welcome.
